# Discovery Plan: Decision confidence before expansion

## Purpose

Northstar is fictional, so this plan describes future discovery rather than reporting findings. The question is whether a product lead can use a compact, traceable metric view to select an investigation without mistaking a rate for a conclusion.

## Unvalidated hypotheses

1. Product and analytics leads will find visible numerators, denominators, and cohort dates more useful than a headline-only KPI when explaining a change.
2. Saving an interpretation with its segment and period will make a follow-up easier to review than an unscoped note.
3. A clear unavailable state will reduce the chance that an empty segment is treated as 0%.

## Recruiting and tasks

Recruit five consenting B2B SaaS product or analytics leads who regularly prepare metric reviews. Use fictional data only. Ask each person to explain one rate, compare two segments, choose what they would investigate next, save a scoped interpretation, and prepare an exported summary. Do not ask them to validate Northstar, the sample counts, or a business outcome.

## What would change the direction

The concept should be reconsidered if participants consistently prefer a simple KPI view after inspecting the traceability controls; cannot explain the cohort observation lag; or cannot tell why the Early access view is unavailable. If scoped notes are not useful in the review exercise, test a lighter handoff mechanism before investing in notebook features. If participants use percentage-point changes as causal proof, strengthen the evidence prompt before expanding the metric set.

The resulting notes could provide directional usability evidence for a product decision. They would not establish market demand, commercial success, or production readiness.
